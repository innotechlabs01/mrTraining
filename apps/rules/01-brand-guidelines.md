## 1. Brand Identity

### Mission

MR Training exists to build the operating system for sports coaching. We decode elite performance and deliver it through software — empowering coaches, athletes, academies, and sports organizations with the intelligence tools to unlock human potential systematically, relentlessly, and at scale.

### Vision

A world where every athlete has access to the same performance intelligence used by champions. Coaching decisions augmented by data. Potential made measurable. The gap between good and great closed by technology.

### Values

- **Relentless Precision** — We obsess over the details that separate average from exceptional. Every feature, every pixel, every interaction is engineered to perform. Margin gains compound.
- **Athlete-First Thinking** — Every decision starts with one question: "Does this make the athlete better?" If the answer is no, we don't build it. The athlete is the north star, not the stakeholder, not the deadline, not the competitor.
- **Velocity Over Perfection** — We ship fast, learn faster, and iterate relentlessly. Speed is a competitive advantage. A shipped feature that's 80% right beats a perfect feature that never ships. We fix forward.
- **Radical Transparency** — Clear data, honest metrics, open communication — with our users and with each other. No dark patterns. No hidden fees. No obscured performance data. Athletes and coaches deserve the unvarnished truth.
- **Engineered Excellence** — We don't settle for "good enough." We build systems that are robust, scalable, and beautiful in their efficiency. Every component has a purpose. Every interaction has intent. Nothing is arbitrary.

### Brand Personality — Six Traits

MR Training speaks with a distinct personality. These six traits define every touchpoint, every sentence, every interaction:

1. **Authoritative** — We speak with the confidence of proven expertise, not arrogance. Our authority comes from data, results, and relentless testing. We don't guess; we know. When MR Training makes a claim, there's a peer-reviewed study, a performance metric, or a coach with twenty years of experience behind it.

2. **Relentless** — We never stop optimizing. Complacency is the enemy of performance, and we apply that standard to ourselves before we ask it of our users. Every release is sharper than the last. Every interaction is faster. Every insight is deeper.

3. **High-Octane** — Our energy is infectious and our ambition is boundless. We believe training is exhilarating, not drudgery. Progress is addictive. The platform should feel like stepping onto the field, not opening a spreadsheet.

4. **Premium** — Quality is non-negotiable. From the materials we choose to the words we write, everything signals craftsmanship. We are not a budget tool. We are not a generic platform. We are the top shelf.

5. **Engineered** — Everything we do is deliberate, calculated, and purposeful. Nothing is random. Our design system has rules. Our voice has principles. Our platform has architecture. The rigor of engineering infuses the brand.

6. **Direct** — We don't waste time. We don't use three words where one will do. We respect the athlete's and coach's time — they have training to do, sessions to run, athletes to develop. Every word we write must earn its place on the screen.

> **The brand archetype:** Apple's precision meets Nike's intensity meets Stripe's sophistication. A premium, high-octane sports brand engineered for performance.

### Brand Promise

MR Training does not just organize coaching data — it amplifies coaching impact. Our promise: **"Every athlete, every coach, every session — sharper."** The platform exists to make the people who use it better at what they do. Coaches coach more effectively. Athletes train with greater purpose. Support professionals collaborate with full context. The platform doesn't replace human judgment; it sharpens it.

## 2. Brand Name

### Origin

"MR Training" stands for **Maximum Results Training**. The name was chosen to reflect the ceiling we help athletes break through — the upper limit of human performance that our platform systematically dismantles. "Maximum Results" is the outcome. The training is the mechanism. The software is the amplifier.

### Pronunciation

Say it as the individual letters: **"Em-Arre Training"** — with a crisp, deliberate enunciation that matches the brand's engineered precision. Never pronounce it as a word ("mur training"). Never say "Mister Training." The separation of the letters conveys authority; mashing them together diminishes it.

### Usage Rules

- Always use "MR Training" as two words with proper capitalization on first reference in body copy.
- Subsequent references may use "MR Training" or "the platform."
- Never abbreviate to just "MR" in customer-facing copy unless used as part of the monogram logo.
- Never pluralize (e.g., "MR Trainings" is incorrect).
- Never use possessive form (e.g., "MR Training's platform" should be rewritten as "the MR Training platform").
- Always set "MR" in uppercase. Never use "Mr Training," "mr training," or "MR training."
- When used as an adjective, hyphenate appropriately: "MR Training-powered analytics," "MR Training-certified coach."
- In code, internal references, and database naming, use lowercase hyphenated form: `mr-training`. Never `mrtraining` or `mr_training`.
- The legal entity name is "MR Training Inc." — used only in legal documents, terms of service, and privacy policies.

### File Naming Conventions

All brand assets, documents, and exported files follow a strict naming convention:

```
[project]-[category]-[descriptor]-[variant]-[size].[ext]
```

Examples:
- `mrtraining-logo-primary-horizontal-1200px.png`
- `mrtraining-logo-monogram-only-256px.svg`
- `mrtraining-icon-navigation-menu-24px.svg`
- `mrtraining-photo-athlete-running-hero-1920x1080.jpg`
- `mrtraining-social-instagram-post-template-1080x1080.psd`
- `mrtraining-font-montserrat-bold.ttf`

Rules:
- Prefix all files with `mrtraining-`.
- Use lowercase only.
- Separate segments with hyphens, never underscores.
- Place the size or dimensions at the end, before the extension.
- Sort order: project → category → descriptor → variant → size.
- Never use spaces, special characters, or camelCase in file names.
- Marketing materials destined for external distribution omit the `mrtraining-` prefix and use the campaign name instead.

## 3. Logo

### Primary Logo

The primary logo lockup consists of two components in a fixed spatial relationship:

1. **MR Monogram** — A bold, geometric ligature of the letters "M" and "R" rendered in Electric Orange (#FF6B00). The letterforms share a common vertical stroke, creating a unified mark that suggests connection, integration, and forward momentum. The monogram is constructed on a strict geometric grid with 45-degree angled cuts at the terminals, giving it a sharp, engineered aesthetic.
2. **TRAINING Wordmark** — Set in Montserrat Bold, weight 700, uppercase, tracked at +100 (100/1000 em), rendered in Deep Black (#0F0F0F) on light backgrounds or White (#FFFFFF) on dark backgrounds.

The monogram sits to the left of the wordmark with a fixed spatial gap equal to the cap height of the "T" in TRAINING. This relationship is locked and may never be altered.

### Logo Variations

| Variation | Composition | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary (Horizontal)** | Monogram + wordmark, horizontal lockup | Default usage. Hero sections, headers, marketing materials. |
| **Stacked** | Monogram centered above wordmark, aligned on their optical centers | When horizontal space is constrained. Event branding, social media profile images, splash screens. |
| **Monogram Only** | MR monogram used standalone | Favicons, app icons, loading spinners, small-scale applications where the wordmark becomes illegible. |
| **Wordmark Only** | "TRAINING" set in Montserrat Bold, uppercase, tracked at +100 | Rarely used. Permitted only when the monogram has already been established in the same context (e.g., footer of a presentation slide where the full logo appears on the title slide). |

### Logo Color Variants

| Variant | Monogram Color | Wordmark Color | Background |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Full Color (Dark BG)** | Electric Orange (#FF6B00) | White (#FFFFFF) | Deep Black (#0F0F0F) or dark surfaces |
| **Full Color (Light BG)** | Electric Orange (#FF6B00) | Deep Black (#0F0F0F) | White (#FFFFFF) or light surfaces |
| **Monochrome (Dark BG)** | White (#FFFFFF) | White (#FFFFFF) | Deep Black (#0F0F0F) or dark surfaces |
| **Monochrome (Light BG)** | Deep Black (#0F0F0F) | Deep Black (#0F0F0F) | White (#FFFFFF) or light surfaces |
| **Reverse** | White (#FFFFFF) | White (#FFFFFF) | Electric Orange (#FF6B00) or Performance Blue (#0066FF) |

The full-color variant is always preferred. Monochrome is a fallback for constrained environments (single-color printing, merchandise engraving, low-contrast display contexts).

### Clear Space

Maintain a minimum clear space around the logo equal to the cap height of the "T" in the TRAINING wordmark — measured from the outermost edges of the lockup. No text, imagery, or graphic elements may encroach on this exclusion zone. The clear space is part of the logo, not empty space around it. Treat it as a protected margin.

For the monogram-only variant, the clear space equals 50% of the monogram's height on all four sides.

### Minimum Size

| Variant | Digital | Print |
|---|---|---|
| **Full Lockup (Horizontal)** | 120px wide | 40mm wide |
| **Stacked Lockup** | 80px wide | 28mm wide |
| **Monogram Only** | 24px tall | 12mm tall |
| **Wordmark Only** | 80px wide | 28mm wide |

These minimums are not suggestions. Rendering the logo below these sizes compromises legibility and brand integrity. For favicons and ultra-small digital applications (16x16px), a simplified single-color monogram may be used — this is the only exception to the minimum size rule.

### Prohibited Uses

The logo is a precision instrument. Treat it as such:

- **Do not** rotate, skew, stretch, or distort the logo in any way. The proportions are fixed.
- **Do not** change the logo colors outside the approved color variants listed above.
- **Do not** add effects — drop shadows, glows, bevels, embossing, gradients, or 3D extrusion.
- **Do not** place the logo on busy photographic backgrounds without sufficient contrast. When placing over photography, use the monochrome variant and ensure at least a 4.5:1 contrast ratio.
- **Do not** rearrange the relationship between monogram and wordmark. The lockup is a single unit.
- **Do not** outline or stroke the logo.
- **Do not** use the logo as a pattern, repeating element, or watermark.
- **Do not** recreate the logo using alternative fonts or typefaces. It is a custom mark, not typed text.
- **Do not** enclose the logo in a shape (circle, square, rounded rectangle) unless the shape is part of an approved brand application (e.g., app icon).
- **Do not** place the logo at an angle. It must always be oriented horizontally, reading left to right.
- **Do not** use the monogram in body copy as a substitute for the brand name. The monogram is a visual mark, not a textual abbreviation.
- **Do not** animate the logo beyond the approved loading spinner (continuous rotation of the monogram only).

The interface is dark-first with a single brand accent: **Volt**. Surfaces ascend
by tonal difference, never by borders alone. Volt is reserved for exactly one
primary CTA per screen. The former Electric Orange (#FF6B00) / Performance Blue
(#0066FF) dual-accent system is retired as of the 2026 mobile redesign.

| Role | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|

| Surface | `#191B1E` | Cards, main content surfaces |
| Surface Raised | `#202329` | Elevated elements, inputs, chips |
| Border | `#26292E` | Hairlines, separators |
| Primary (Volt) | `#C8FF00` | The single accent. One primary CTA per screen. Progress fills, active indicators. |

| Text Secondary | `#9CA3AF` | Secondary text, captions, placeholders |

| Warning | `#FBBF24` | Caution states, pending membership |

### Usage Ratios

| Category | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Neutral surfaces and text (Base → Border, Text roles) | ≥ 90% |
| Volt accent (CTAs, active states, key brand moments) | ≤ 10% |

### Color Rules

- Volt is the ONLY accent. Never introduce a second hue for decoration. Semantic
  colors (Success/Warning/Error) appear only for state communication.
- Exactly one Volt CTA per screen. Secondary actions are ghost/outline treatments.
- Functional colors always pair with an icon or label — color is reinforcement,
  never the sole communication channel.
- All text must meet WCAG AA contrast against its background. Body text on Volt
  must be Base (`#111214`), never white.
- Gradients: permitted only between adjacent tonal steps of the same neutral ramp.
  Never gradient into or out of Volt.

## 5. Typography

### Primary Typeface: Montserrat

Montserrat is the display and headline typeface. Its geometric, high-contrast letterforms convey authority, modernity, and engineered precision. The uppercase athletic style is a signature of the brand — bold, commanding, and unmistakably sports-oriented.

- **Styles loaded:** Medium (500), SemiBold (600), Bold (700), ExtraBold (800)
- **Use case:** Headlines, navigation, labels, buttons, overlines, display text
- **Uppercase athletic style:** Headlines at H2 and above should be set in uppercase when the headline is 6 words or fewer. Montserrat's uppercase letterforms have the weight and proportion to carry an all-caps treatment without feeling aggressive.
- **Never use:** Light (300) or Thin (100) weights. The brand has no need for delicate typography.
- **Character:** Geometric, urban, modern. The typeface itself communicates the brand personality.

### Secondary Typeface: Inter

Inter is the body and interface typeface. Purpose-built for screens, it delivers exceptional legibility at small sizes and maintains clarity across the full range of UI contexts — dense data tables, long-form athlete notes, form labels, and metric displays.

- **Styles loaded:** Regular (400), Medium (500), SemiBold (600)
- **Use case:** Body copy, data tables, form inputs, labels, captions, long-form reading, metric labels
- **Never use:** Thin (100) or Black (900) weights in body copy. SemiBold is the maximum emphasis weight for body text; Bold in Inter is reserved for metric values only.
- **Character:** Clean, neutral, highly readable. Inter stays out of the way so the content speaks.

### Monospace Typeface: JetBrains Mono

Used exclusively for code blocks, technical identifiers, and raw data values that benefit from monospaced alignment.

- **Styles loaded:** Regular (400), Medium (500)
- **Use case:** Code snippets, API references, exported data views, technical documentation
- **Fallback stack:** `'JetBrains Mono', 'Fira Code', 'Cascadia Code', 'Consolas', monospace`

### Fallback Stacks

```css
--font-display: 'Montserrat', -apple-system, 'Helvetica Neue', 'Arial Black', sans-serif;
--font-body: 'Inter', -apple-system, 'Segoe UI', 'Roboto', 'Helvetica Neue', sans-serif;
--font-mono: 'JetBrains Mono', 'Fira Code', 'Cascadia Code', 'Consolas', 'Courier New', monospace;
```

The fallback stacks are designed to maintain approximate visual character when web fonts fail to load. System sans-serifs that trend geometric (Helvetica Neue, Arial Black) follow Montserrat. System sans-serifs that trend humanist/neutral (Segoe UI, Roboto) follow Inter.

### Type Scale

All type is set on a modular scale with a ratio of 1.25 (major third), rounded to the nearest integer pixel. A separate mobile scale applies below 768px viewport width.

| Level | Family | Weight | Size (Desktop) | Size (Mobile) | Line Height | Letter Spacing | Text Transform |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display | Montserrat | ExtraBold (800) | 48px | 32px | 1.1 (53px) | -0.02em | uppercase |
| H1 | Montserrat | Bold (700) | 36px | 28px | 1.2 (43px) | -0.01em | uppercase |
| H2 | Montserrat | Bold (700) | 28px | 24px | 1.25 (35px) | -0.01em | uppercase |
| H3 | Montserrat | SemiBold (600) | 22px | 20px | 1.3 (29px) | 0 | sentence case |
| H4 | Montserrat | SemiBold (600) | 18px | 16px | 1.35 (24px) | 0 | sentence case |
| Body Large | Inter | Regular (400) | 18px | 16px | 1.6 (29px) | 0 | sentence case |
| Body | Inter | Regular (400) | 16px | 14px | 1.6 (26px) | 0 | sentence case |
| Body Small | Inter | Regular (400) | 14px | 12px | 1.5 (21px) | 0.01em | sentence case |
| Caption | Inter | Medium (500) | 12px | 11px | 1.4 (17px) | 0.02em | sentence case |
| Overline | Montserrat | Medium (500) | 11px | 10px | 1.4 (15px) | 0.1em | uppercase |
| Code | JetBrains Mono | Regular (400) | 14px | 13px | 1.5 (21px) | 0 | as-is |

### Typography Rules

- **Headings form a strict hierarchy.** Never skip levels (e.g., H2 to H4 without an H3 in between). Every page has exactly one H1. This is non-negotiable for accessibility and SEO.
- **Maximum line length for body copy:** 75 characters. Paragraphs exceeding this width must be constrained with `max-width: 65ch`.
- **All-caps rules:** Reserved exclusively for overlines, the TRAINING wordmark, and headlines at H2 and above when the headline is 6 words or fewer. Never use all-caps for body copy, even for emphasis. All-caps body text is unreadable and aggressive.
- **Typeface boundaries:** Montserrat is never used below 11px. Inter is never used below 12px (mobile) or 14px (desktop) for body copy. These minimums exist to preserve legibility.
- **Alignment:** Text must always be left-aligned. Center alignment is permitted only for short headlines of 5 words or fewer, and only in hero sections, empty states, and confirmation modals.
- **Weight restraint:** Never use more than three font weights in a single composition. The combination of 800 (display), 700 (heading), 600 (subheading), 500 (overline), and 400 (body) within one page is acceptable because they serve distinct hierarchical roles. But adding Light (300) or Black (900) introduces visual noise without adding meaning.
- **Monospace exclusivity:** JetBrains Mono is used exclusively for code, technical identifiers, and raw data values. Never use it for body copy or UI labels. Conversely, never use Inter or Montserrat for code blocks.

## 6. Iconography

### Style

All MR Training icons are **stroke-based** with a consistent **2px stroke weight**. Icons use rounded caps and rounded joins for a modern, approachable aesthetic that softens the otherwise sharp, engineered visual language. The stroke-based approach ensures visual consistency with the data-driven nature of the platform and maintains clarity at all sizes.

The uniform stroke weight is non-negotiable. No icon may use a stroke weight other than 2px. No icon may mix stroke-based and fill-based shapes within the same icon.

### Grid System

Icons are designed on a **24x24px grid** with a 2px padding on all sides (live area: **20x20px**). This ensures optical consistency across all icon shapes — circular, square, and rectangular — preventing any single icon from appearing visually heavier or lighter than its neighbors.

- Grid: 24x24px
- Live area: 20x20px
- Stroke: 2px
- Corner radius: default rounded (2px), sharp where semantically appropriate (e.g., data table icons)

### Color Modes

Icons must always be rendered in a single flat color. The appropriate color is determined by context:

| Context | Color | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Default (dark mode) | `#C4C4C4` (Fog) | Inactive navigation, toolbar icons, secondary actions |
| Active (dark mode) | `#FFFFFF` (White) | Active navigation, selected items |
| Default (light mode) | `#4A4A4A` (Steel) | Inactive navigation, toolbar icons |
| Active (light mode) | `#0F0F0F` (Deep Black) | Active navigation, selected items |
| Brand accent | `#FF6B00` (Electric Orange) | Primary action icons, active states |
| Interactive | `#0066FF` (Performance Blue) | Clickable icons, info tooltips |
| Success | `#00C853` (Success Green) | Checkmarks, completion indicators |
| Warning | `#FFB300` (Warning Amber) | Caution indicators |
| Error | `#FF3D00` (Error Red) | Error indicators, destructive actions |

### Icon Categories

Every icon in the MR Training library belongs to one of these categories:

| Category | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Actions** | User-initiated operations | Add, edit, delete, save, export, share, duplicate, download, upload, copy, paste, refresh, undo, redo |
| **Sports** | Sport-specific and athletic concepts | Gym/dumbbell, running/shoe, tennis/racket, swimming/pool, cycling/bike, CrossFit/kettlebell, athlete, coach, team, competition, trophy, medal, podium |
| **Navigation** | Wayfinding and orientation | Menu, back, forward, close, search, filter, sort, home, chevron (up/down/left/right), arrow, external link, expand, collapse |
| **Status** | System and data states | Check, alert, info, error, warning, success, pending, locked, unlocked, verified, synced, offline, online |
| **Equipment** | Training apparatus and gear | Barbell, dumbbell, kettlebell, resistance band, jump rope, medicine ball, foam roller, stopwatch, whistle, clipboard, tablet, heart rate monitor, GPS watch |
| **Data** | Analytics and metrics | Chart bar, chart line, chart pie (donut), trend up, trend down, trend flat, target, goal, timer, calendar, clock, ruler, scale, speedometer |
| **Communication** | Messaging and collaboration | Notification, message, email, comment, mention, announcement, broadcast, video call, voice note, attachment |
| **File & Document** | Content management | Document, folder, image, video, PDF, spreadsheet, presentation, file add, file search |

### Icon Font

The icon library is delivered as an icon font for web usage and as SVG assets for native applications. The icon font uses the following conventions:

- **Font name:** `mrtraining-icons`
- **Glyph mapping:** Private Use Area (U+E000 through U+E7FF), ensuring no collision with standard character sets.
- **Ligatures:** Supported for development convenience. Typing `:athlete:` in a code editor renders the athlete icon.
- **CSS class prefix:** `.mrti-` (e.g., `<i class="mrti mrti-athlete"></i>`).
- **Sizing:** The icon font is designed at 24px base size. Sizing is controlled via `font-size` in CSS — never via `transform: scale()`.

### Icon Usage Rules

- Icons must always be rendered in a single color as specified in the color modes table above.
- Minimum icon size is 16x16px. Recommended default size is 24x24px. Large icons for hero sections and empty states may use 48x48px or 64x64px, but these must be purpose-designed variants, not scaled-up 24px icons.
- When paired with text labels, icons must maintain a minimum 8px gap from the label (measured from the icon's bounding box, not its live area).
- Icons must never replace words in buttons, navigation items, or CTAs without accompanying text labels. The only exceptions are universally recognized icons (close X, search magnifying glass, hamburger menu) and icons in the collapsed sidebar and bottom navigation bar.
- Never mix filled and outlined icon styles within the same view. The stroke-based style is mandatory across the entire platform.
- Icon-button touch targets must be a minimum of 44x44px, even when the icon itself is 24x24px. The additional padding is part of the interactive element.

## 7. Photography

### Style Direction

MR Training photography is **high-contrast, athletic, and cinematic**. Every image should convey intensity, focus, and the visceral reality of training. We capture athletes in their element — not posing, not smiling at the camera, not staged in unrealistic scenarios. The camera is a witness to effort, not a director of it.

Think sports documentary, not sports commercial. Grain and grit are acceptable. Perfection is not the goal — authenticity is.

### Lighting

- **Dramatic side lighting** with strong, directional shadows. Light should carve the subject out of the frame, not flatten them.
- **High contrast ratios** — minimum 4:1 between highlights and shadows. Silhouettes and rim lighting are encouraged for hero images.
- **Warm tones** (2700K–4000K) for indoor/training environments — gym lighting, locker rooms, indoor tracks, pools under artificial light.
- **Cool tones** (5500K–7500K) for outdoor/competition settings — natural daylight, stadium floodlights, early morning training, twilight sessions.
- **Avoid flat, even lighting** at all costs. If a photo looks like it was taken with a ring light, it does not belong in the MR Training brand.
- **One light source per image.** The viewer should be able to identify where the light is coming from. Two competing light sources create visual confusion.

### Composition

- **Tight framing on the subject.** Get close. Fill the frame with the athlete, the equipment, the effort. Wide shots are only acceptable when establishing context (stadium, field, training facility).
- **Shallow depth of field** (f/1.4–f/2.8) to isolate subjects and separate them from backgrounds.
- **Dynamic angles:**
  - **Low angles** convey power, dominance, aspiration. Shoot from below the athlete's eye line.
  - **Eye-level** conveys connection, humanity, relatability. Use for coach-athlete interactions and intimate training moments.
  - **Dutch angles** (slight tilt, 2–10 degrees) convey energy and motion. Use sparingly — no more than 20% of images.
- **Leading lines** that guide the eye toward the subject or action — lane lines on a track, pool lane dividers, gym flooring patterns, tennis court lines.
- **Negative space** is acceptable and encouraged when used intentionally for text overlay. Hero images should provide a clear zone for headline placement. The subject should occupy either the left or right 60% of the frame, leaving 40% for copy.

### Subject Matter

- **Athletes in peak action moments:** Mid-stride running, mid-lift under a barbell, mid-serve on a tennis court, mid-stroke in a pool. Never posed. Never smiling at the camera. Always in the act of performing.
- **Coaches in focused observation or instruction:** Looking at data on a tablet, gesturing to an athlete, studying a whiteboard, reviewing video footage. The coach as analyst, not cheerleader.
- **Training environments:** Gyms (the grittier the better), outdoor tracks, pools, tennis courts, cycling roads, CrossFit boxes, sports fields. The environment should feel like a place where work gets done.
- **Technology in context:** Tablets on sidelines during practice, smartwatches on wrists mid-workout, dashboards displayed on monitors in coaching offices, data overlays on training footage. Technology as an integrated tool, not a prop.
- **Details of training:** Close-ups of chalk on hands, weight plates, starting blocks, pool lane markers, sweat, focused eyes, taped wrists, worn-out training shoes, stopwatches, heart rate monitor straps. These detail shots provide texture and atmosphere.

### Color Treatment

- **Desaturated backgrounds** with the subject retaining natural saturation and contrast. The athlete pops; the environment recedes. Achieve this through a combination of lighting and selective color grading.
- **Electric Orange or Performance Blue color grading** applied as subtle accent overlays (5–10% opacity adjustments in shadows or highlights). Never apply both colors in the same image.
- **High-contrast black and white** is acceptable for hero images, atmospheric shots, and editorial content. Black and white images should push contrast further than color images — aim for true blacks and clean whites, not mid-gray mush.
- **Never apply:** Heavy filters, vintage effects, Instagram-style presets, lens flares, excessive vignetting, or any treatment that feels like social media rather than professional sports media.

### Pose Library

For controlled studio and promotional photography, maintain a pose library of approved athlete positions:

| Category | Poses |
|---|---|
| **Power & Strength** | Standing with arms crossed (coach authority), mid-deadlift lockout, overhead press finish, front squat bottom position |
| **Speed & Agility** | Starting block stance, mid-sprint stride (both feet off ground), lateral cut/change of direction, hurdle clearance |
| **Endurance** | Runner in stride (side profile), cyclist climbing out of saddle, swimmer mid-stroke breathing |
| **Precision** | Tennis serve trophy position, golf swing follow-through, basketball free-throw release |
| **Recovery & Focus** | Athlete stretching (seated hamstring, standing quad), foam rolling, meditation/breathing, reviewing data on tablet |
| **Coach-Athlete** | Coach pointing at data with athlete listening, coach demonstrating movement, shared laugh after session, coach adjusting athlete's form |

### Photography Restrictions

- No images that depict unsafe training practices (rounded back deadlifts, locked-out knees under load, unsupervised spotting).
- No images with visible brand logos other than MR Training. All competitor branding must be obscured or cropped out.
- No overly sexualized or objectifying imagery. Athletes are portrayed as powerful, capable, and focused — never as decorative.
- No images that reinforce harmful stereotypes about gender, race, body type, age, or ability. Sports photography is for everyone.
- All athletes must appear focused, determined, and empowered. No images of defeated, humiliated, or injured athletes.
- No AI-generated photography. All images must feature real athletes in real training environments. Authenticity is non-negotiable.
- Model releases must be secured for all identifiable individuals in photography used for commercial purposes.

## 8. Voice & Tone

### Writing Style

MR Training writes like a coach who respects the athlete's time: **bold, direct, and relentless — never cheesy, never corny, never cringe.** We don't do motivational posters. We don't do hollow slogans. We don't tell people to "unleash their inner beast" or "crush it." We tell them what the data says, what they should do about it, and why it matters — in as few words as possible.

- **Active voice, always.** "Track your progress" not "Your progress can be tracked."
- **Present tense, always.** "The system analyzes" not "The system will analyze."
- **Be specific.** "Improve your 40-yard dash by 0.3 seconds in 8 weeks" not "Get faster." Vague promises erode trust; specific claims build it.
- **Be concise.** If a sentence can be shorter, make it shorter. If a word can be cut, cut it. Every word must earn its place on the screen.
- **Use "you" and "your"** to speak directly to the user. The platform is personal, and the language should reflect that.
- **Avoid jargon** unless it's sports-specific terminology the audience already knows. "Periodization" is fine. "Synergistic optimization of longitudinal performance vectors" is not.
- **Never use exclamation points** in body copy or UI text. Exclamation points signal desperation, not enthusiasm. An exception is made for celebratory milestone moments ("New PR!") where the exclamation is earned by actual achievement data.
- **Never use the word "journey"** to describe athletic development. It's training. It's work. It's a process. "Journey" is what yoga retreats sell.
- **Never use the word "unleash," "beast," "savage," "grind," or "hustle"** in any MR Training copy. These are clichés of the worst kind. The brand is engineered, not performative.
- **Never use emoji** in product interface copy. Emoji are permitted in social media and marketing contexts only.

### Headline Formulas

MR Training headlines follow one of three proven structures:

1. **Verb-First (Action-Focused):**
   - "Build championship programs."
   - "Track every rep. Analyze every trend."
   - "Coach with clarity. Train with purpose."
   - "Stop guessing. Start knowing."
   - "Program smarter. Perform better."

2. **Data-Driven (Metric-Focused):**
   - "12% faster sprints in 8 weeks."
   - "3.2x more athlete insights per session."
   - "Zero missed workouts. Full accountability."
   - "Every metric. Every athlete. One platform."

3. **Challenge-Based (Tension-Focused):**
   - "Your data deserves better than a spreadsheet."
   - "Coaching without limits."
   - "The gap between good and great is measurable."
   - "Your athletes are evolving. Your tools should too."

Headlines should never exceed 8 words. The optimal headline is 4–6 words with a strong verb and a concrete outcome.

### Microcopy Guidelines

Microcopy is the smallest text in the interface — buttons, labels, helper text, empty states, error messages, confirmation dialogs. It carries outsized weight because it appears at moments of decision and friction.

#### Buttons

- Use verb-first, action-oriented language. Tell the user exactly what will happen.
- **Good:** "Start Workout" / "Save Program" / "View Report" / "Add Athlete" / "Complete Session"
- **Bad:** "Submit" / "Click Here" / "OK" / "Done" (too vague)
- Button text should be 1–3 words. If you need 4 words, the action may be too complex for a single button.
- Primary destructive actions (delete, remove, cancel subscription) use the label format: "Delete [Noun]" — "Delete Athlete," "Remove Program," "Cancel Subscription." The noun makes the action unambiguous.
- Loading state: Replace button text with a spinner. Do not use "Loading..." text.

#### Form Labels

- Be specific. The user should never wonder what information goes in a field.
- **Good:** "Athlete's full name" / "Season start date" / "Weekly mileage target" / "Resting heart rate"
- **Bad:** "Name" / "Date" / "Target" / "RHR"
- Required fields are marked with an asterisk (*) in Error Red (#FF3D00) after the label text.
- Optional fields are marked with "(optional)" in `--color-text-tertiary` after the label text.

#### Empty States

Be encouraging but not saccharine. Acknowledge the void and show the path forward.

- **Good:** "No sessions logged yet. Create your first workout and start tracking."
- **Good:** "Your dashboard is ready. Add athletes to see their performance at a glance."
- **Good:** "No messages yet. Send your coach a question or check-in."
- **Bad:** "No data." / "Nothing here." / "You don't have any workouts." (Blames the user or adds no value.)

#### Success Messages

Be specific and affirming. Tell the user what happened and what it means.

- **Good:** "Session saved. 14 athletes logged successfully."
- **Good:** "Program published. Your athletes will see it in their dashboard immediately."
- **Good:** "Payment received. $149.00 — next invoice: July 1, 2026."
- **Bad:** "Saved." / "Success." / "Done."

#### Error Messages

Error messages must be helpful, not hostile. Every error message should:
1. State what happened in plain language.
2. Explain what the user can do to fix it.
3. Avoid technical jargon and error codes (unless the user is a developer viewing an API response).
4. Never blame the user.

- **Good:** "We couldn't save that session. Check your connection and try again."
- **Good:** "That email is already associated with another account. Try a different email or contact your admin."
- **Good:** "File too large. Videos must be under 500 MB. Try compressing or trimming the clip."
- **Bad:** "Error 503: Internal Server Error." / "Invalid input." / "Something went wrong."

#### CTAs (Calls to Action)

- Four-word maximum.
- Verb-first.
- Promise value, not process.
- **Good:** "Start Your Free Trial" / "Build Your First Program" / "See Athlete Performance" / "Get Started — Free"
- **Bad:** "Learn More" (learn what?), "Click Here" (why?), "Sign Up Now" (for what?)

Data visualizations use a dedicated palette derived from but distinct from the brand palette. Chart colors must be distinguishable by users with color vision deficiency (deuteranopia, protanopia, tritanopia).

| Series | Color | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary series | Electric Orange | `#FF6B00` | Main data series, primary metric |
| Secondary series | Performance Blue | `#0066FF` | Comparison data, secondary metric |
| Tertiary series | Teal | `#00BFA5` | Third data series, auxiliary metric |
| Quaternary series | Warning Amber | `#FFB300` | Fourth data series |
| Quinary series | Violet | `#7C4DFF` | Fifth data series |
| Positive trend | Success Green | `#00C853` | Upward trends, targets exceeded |
| Negative trend | Error Red | `#FF3D00` | Downward trends, targets missed |
| Neutral trend | Steel | `#4A4A4A` | Flat trends, no change |

### Chart Elements

| Element | Color (Dark Mode) | Color (Light Mode) | Specification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grid lines | `#2A2A2C` | `#D4D4D4` | 1px solid, no dashes |
| Axis lines | `#4A4A4A` | `#8A8A8A` | 1px solid |
| Axis labels | `#8A8A8A` | `#4A4A4A` | Inter, 12px, Regular |
| Data point dots | Match series color | Match series color | 6px diameter, 1px white stroke |

| Annotations | `#FF6B00` | `#FF6B00` | Dashed line, 1px, with label at end |

### Chart Types and Specifications

#### Bar Charts
- Used for comparing discrete categories.
- Bar width: minimum 12px, maximum 48px.
- Gap between bars: 25% of bar width.
- Top corners rounded at 4px (`--radius-sm`).
- Horizontal bar charts for category labels longer than 15 characters.
- Vertical bar charts for time-series comparisons (monthly, weekly).

#### Line Charts
- Used for trends over time.
- Line stroke weight: 2px.
- Data point dots: 6px diameter at each data point.
- Smooth curves (`curveMonotoneX`) preferred over sharp angles (`curveLinear`).
- Gradient fill below the line: series color at 8% opacity fading to 0%.
- Maximum 4 lines on a single chart. Beyond 4, use small multiples.

#### Area Charts
- Used for volume or cumulative data.
- Fill opacity: 10% of the series color.
- Stacked areas: maximum 3 series. Beyond 3, use a line chart.
- Baseline always at zero. Never truncate the y-axis on an area chart — area height is a visual encoding of value, and truncation distorts this encoding.

#### Donut Charts
- Used for proportional data (parts of a whole).
- Inner radius: 60% of outer radius.
- Segment gap: 2px.
- Center: display the total value in Montserrat Bold, 24px, with the unit label in Inter Regular, 12px below.
- Maximum 5 segments. Beyond 5, group the smallest segments into an "Other" category.
- Never use pie charts. The donut's center hole provides a space for the total value label, which pie charts lack.

#### Heatmaps
- Used for distribution and density data.
- Color scale: Deep Black (#0F0F0F) → Charcoal (#1C1C1C) → Steel (#4A4A4A) → Electric Orange (#FF6B00) → Performance Blue (#0066FF).
- Cell border: 2px `--color-surface-0` (creates gutters between cells).
- Cell hover: 1px white border.
- Include a gradient legend scale.

### Metric Cards

Metric cards are the atomic unit of the MR Training dashboard. They display a single number with context.

**Anatomy:**
```
+---------------------------------------+
| LABEL           [sparkline or icon]    |  Overline, 11px, Montserrat Medium
|                                       |
| 128.4                                 |  Primary value, 36px, Montserrat ExtraBold
| kg                                     |  Unit, 14px, Inter Regular, Steel
|                                       |
| ▲ 12.3% vs last week                   |  Trend, 12px, Inter Medium
+---------------------------------------+
```

- **Label:** `--text-overline` style. Uppercase, tracked, Montserrat Medium, 11px, `--color-text-secondary`.
- **Primary value:** Montserrat ExtraBold, 36px minimum, `--color-text-primary`. Right-aligned within card for numerical data. Left-aligned for text-based metrics.
- **Unit:** Inter Regular, 14px, `--color-text-tertiary`. Placed immediately after the primary value without a space break if the unit is a symbol (kg, lb, mi, km, %). Space break if the unit is a word.
- **Trend indicator:**
  - Positive (upward): Green arrow (▲) in Success Green + percentage/absolute change.
  - Negative (downward): Red arrow (▼) in Error Red + percentage/absolute change.
  - Neutral (flat): Gray dash (—) in Steel + "No change."
- **Sparkline:** Optional. Placed in the top-right of the card. A miniature line chart (80px wide, 24px tall) showing the last 7–14 data points. No axes, no gridlines, just the trend line at 1px stroke in `--color-text-tertiary`. The most recent point is Electric Orange.

### Progress Indicators

- **Circular progress rings:** 4px stroke, rounded caps. Track color: `--color-surface-5`. Fill: gradient or solid Electric Orange. Center: percentage value in Montserrat Bold, 16px. Minimum diameter: 48px.
- **Linear progress bars:** 4px height, 4px corner radius (`--radius-sm`). Track: `--color-surface-5`. Fill: Electric Orange by default, Success Green for completed, Warning Amber for approaching limits, Error Red for exceeded thresholds.
- Always show the numerical value alongside visual progress. Progress is data, not decoration.
- Animate progress indicators on load: fill animates from 0 to target value over 800ms using the standard easing curve.

## 10. Motion & Animation

### Duration Tokens

Animation durations are standardized into five tiers. Every animation in the product must use one of these durations. No custom durations. No arbitrary timing.

| Token | Duration | CSS Variable | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instant** | 0ms | `--duration-instant` | State changes where motion would add latency (toggles, checkboxes, switches) |
| **Micro** | 150ms | `--duration-micro` | Button state changes, hover transitions, icon state swaps, focus ring appearance |
| **Fast** | 250ms | `--duration-fast` | Small UI transitions, fade-ins, tooltip appearance, dropdown open/close |
| **Standard** | 350ms | `--duration-standard` | Panel transitions, modal open/close, page transitions, card expansions |
| **Slow** | 500ms | `--duration-slow` | Complex animations, hero section reveals, dashboard load sequences |
| **Dramatic** | 800ms | `--duration-dramatic` | Page-load sequences, celebratory milestone moments, onboarding transitions |

### Easing Curves

| Name | Cubic Bezier | CSS Variable | Character | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)` | `--ease-standard` | Smooth deceleration | Default for most transitions. Asymmetric — fast start, gentle finish. |
| **Enter** | `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1)` | `--ease-enter` | Strong deceleration | Elements appearing on screen. Rapid entry, smooth arrival. |
| **Exit** | `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1)` | `--ease-exit` | Strong acceleration | Elements leaving the screen. Slow start, rapid exit. |
| **Sharp** | `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.6, 1)` | `--ease-sharp` | Symmetrical, precise | Snappy interactions, toggles, switches, checkboxes. No overshoot. |
| **Spring** | `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)` | `--ease-spring` | Bouncy overshoot | Playful micro-interactions, celebration moments, pull-to-refresh. |

### Animation Types

#### Fade
- **Usage:** Content replacement, state transitions, element reveal/hide.
- **Duration:** `--duration-fast` (250ms) or `--duration-standard` (350ms) for larger elements.
- **Properties:** `opacity` only. Never combine with `transform` in a fade — that's a different animation.
- **Rule:** Fade is the default. When in doubt, fade. It's the most restrained, least distracting transition.

#### Slide
- **Usage:** Directional movement — drawers, carousels, navigation transitions, notification panels.
- **Duration:** `--duration-standard` (350ms).
- **Properties:** `transform: translateX()` or `transform: translateY()`, combined with `opacity` (fade + slide).
- **Directions:** Left (back navigation, drawer close), Right (drawer open, forward navigation), Up (bottom sheets, keyboard appearance), Down (dismissal, closing).
- **Rule:** Slide direction must match the conceptual model. Content coming from the right slides in from the right. Content being dismissed slides out the direction it came from.

#### Scale
- **Usage:** Modals, dialogs, popovers, emphasis interactions.
- **Duration:** `--duration-standard` (350ms).
- **Properties:** `transform: scale()` from 0.95 to 1.0, combined with `opacity` from 0 to 1. Uses `--ease-spring` for a subtle bounce at the end that signals "I'm here, pay attention."
- **Rule:** Scale animation is reserved for elements that appear centered in the viewport and demand attention. Never scale side panels or inline elements.

#### Stagger
- **Usage:** Lists, card grids, grouped elements appearing sequentially.
- **Duration:** `--duration-standard` (350ms) per item, staggered by 50ms intervals.
- **Implementation:** Each child element's animation is delayed by `index × 50ms`. Maximum 10 children — after 10, all remaining children animate simultaneously.
- **Rule:** Stagger animations communicate "there are multiple things here, each distinct." They guide the eye from top to bottom. Never stagger more than 20 items.

#### Morph
- **Usage:** Related-state transitions where the same element changes form (list item expanding to detail view, icon swapping to a different icon, card transitioning between sizes).
- **Duration:** `--duration-slow` (500ms).
- **Properties:** Shared element transitions using FLIP (First, Last, Invert, Play) technique or view transitions API.
- **Rule:** Morph animations are the most complex and should be used sparingly. Reserve for signature interactions that define the product experience.

### Motion Principles

- **Every animation must serve a purpose.** The purpose can be one of three things: guide attention, provide feedback, or convey a state change. Never animate for decoration alone. Animation that doesn't serve a purpose is performance overhead and cognitive noise.
- **Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.** When the user's operating system preference is set to reduce motion, disable all non-essential animations. Replace animated transitions with instant state changes or simple opacity fades (150ms maximum). Essential animations are those required to understand a state change — a progress bar filling, a loading spinner, a notification appearing. Non-essential animations are decorative reveals, parallax effects, staggered lists, and animated illustrations.
- **Animation is information, not entertainment.** The MR Training platform is a professional tool. Animations should feel precise, crisp, and purposeful — not playful, bouncy, or gimmicky. The spring easing curve is the one exception, used for celebratory moments where earned joy is appropriate.
- **No content may flash more than three times per second.** This is both a WCAG compliance requirement and a basic decency standard.
- **Scroll-triggered animations** are permitted on marketing pages and landing pages only. Never use scroll-triggered animations within the product interface.

### Loading Animations

- **MR Monogram Spinner:** The primary loading indicator is the MR monogram rotating continuously at 720rpm (one full rotation per ~83ms). The rotation is clockwise and continuous. The monogram is rendered in Electric Orange at 24px height. This spinner replaces the browser-default spinner in all contexts.
- **Skeleton Screens:** Pulse animation for placeholder content. Opacity oscillates between 0.3 and 0.7 over a 1.5s cycle using `--ease-standard`. Skeleton shapes mirror the final content layout exactly. Respects `prefers-reduced-motion` — becomes static when motion is reduced.
- **Determinate Progress:** Linear progress bars animate from 0% to the target value over `--duration-dramatic` (800ms) using `--ease-standard`. The fill color transitions from Electric Orange to Success Green when reaching 100%.

## 11. Accessibility

### Compliance Target

MR Training targets **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** compliance across all digital products. Where AAA compliance is achievable without compromising the brand's visual identity, it is pursued. The minimum bar is AA; the aspiration is AAA.

### Contrast Ratios

All text must meet the following minimum contrast ratios against its background:

| Element | Requirement | Foreground | Background (Dark) | Ratio | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Body text (large, ≥18px or bold ≥14px) | AA: 3:1 | `#FFFFFF` | `#0F0F0F` | 18.9:1 | AAA |
| Body text (normal, <18px) | AA: 4.5:1 | `#FFFFFF` | `#0F0F0F` | 18.9:1 | AAA |
| Secondary text (captions, metadata) | AA: 4.5:1 | `#C4C4C4` | `#0F0F0F` | 6.2:1 | AA |
| Tertiary text (placeholders) | AA: 4.5:1 | `#8A8A8A` | `#0F0F0F` | 3.5:1 | **Does not meet AA** — use only for decorative/non-essential text |
| CTA text on orange | AA: 4.5:1 | `#0F0F0F` | `#FF6B00` | 3.1:1 | Use dark text on orange buttons. White text on orange fails AA. |
| CTA text on orange (dark text) | AA: 4.5:1 | `#0F0F0F` | `#FF6B00` | 5.3:1 | AA |
| Link text | AA: 4.5:1 | `#3D8BFF` (lightened blue) | `#0F0F0F` | 5.2:1 | AA |
| Focus ring (blue) | 2:1 against adjacent | `#0066FF` | `#0F0F0F` | 4.6:1 | **Passes** |
| Error text on dark | AA: 4.5:1 | `#FF5252` (lightened red) | `#0F0F0F` | 5.1:1 | AA |
| Success text on dark | AA: 4.5:1 | `#00E676` (lightened green) | `#0F0F0F` | 6.8:1 | AA |

**Critical note:** `#8A8A8A` (Steel) text on `#0F0F0F` (Deep Black) has a contrast ratio of only 3.5:1 — it does not meet AA for normal text. This color must only be used for:
- Decorative text elements
- Disabled text (exempt from contrast requirements per WCAG)
- Visual elements that are not text content

For placeholder text that provides essential information, use `#C4C4C4` (Fog) instead, which achieves 6.2:1.

### Focus States

- All interactive elements must have a visible, high-contrast focus indicator.
- **Focus ring specification:** 2px solid Performance Blue (#0066FF) with a 2px offset from the element boundary. Total visual impact: 2px blue ring at 2px distance.
- **Fallback for Windows High Contrast Mode:** Use a transparent outline with `outline: 2px solid transparent` as the default state and `outline-color: #0066FF` on `:focus-visible`. This ensures the focus ring is visible in WHCM even when custom styles are overridden.
- Never remove focus outlines with `outline: none` without providing an equivalent, high-visibility alternative.
- Focus ring must have a minimum 2:1 contrast ratio against all adjacent colors.
- Keyboard navigation must be supported for all interactive elements in a logical tab order that follows the visual layout.

### Screen Reader Considerations

- All images must have meaningful `alt` text that describes the content and function of the image. Decorative images use `alt=""` (empty string).
- Form inputs must have associated `<label>` elements linked via `for`/`id` attributes. Placeholder text is never a substitute for a label.
- Interactive elements must have descriptive `aria-label` attributes where visible labels are insufficient or absent (icon-only buttons, close buttons, collapsed sidebar items).
- Page structure must use proper heading hierarchy (H1 → H2 → H3, never skip levels). Landmark regions must be identified (`<main>`, `<nav>`, `<aside>`, `<header>`, `<footer>`).
- Live regions (`aria-live="polite"` or `aria-live="assertive"`) must be used for dynamic content updates — real-time data, notifications, loading states, error messages.
- Skip navigation links must be provided on all pages as the first focusable element.
- Focus management: when a modal opens, focus moves to the first focusable element inside it. When it closes, focus returns to the trigger element. When a route changes, focus moves to the H1 of the new page or a skiplink target.

### Color Independence

- Never use color as the sole means of conveying information. Any information communicated through color must also be communicated through another channel — icons, text labels, patterns, or shapes.
- Error states: red color + error icon (alert triangle) + error message text.
- Success states: green color + success icon (checkmark) + success message text.
- Warning states: amber color + warning icon (warning triangle) + warning text.
- Data visualizations: pair distinct colors with distinct dash patterns for line charts, distinct hatch patterns for bar and area charts, or direct labels. Test all charts using a color blindness simulator before shipping.
- Links: must be distinguishable from body text by more than color alone. MR Training links are underlined on hover and focus, and use a distinct typeface weight (Medium vs Regular body text).

### Motion Sensitivity

- All animations must respect the `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` media query.
- When reduced motion is enabled, replace all animations with instant state changes or simple opacity fades of 150ms or less.
- No content may flash more than three times per second — this is both a WCAG requirement and essential for users with photosensitive epilepsy.
- Auto-playing video and audio must not occur without explicit user interaction. Video players must have visible, keyboard-accessible controls.

### Touch Targets

- Minimum touch target size: **44x44px** (WCAG 2.5.5, Level AAA recommendation).
- Minimum spacing between touch targets: **8px**.
- This applies to all interactive elements: buttons, links, form inputs, toggles, checkboxes, dropdown triggers, icon buttons, pagination controls.
- In dense interfaces (data tables, compact cards), touch targets may reduce to 32x32px if the element is not the primary interaction method on mobile, but this should be the exception, not the rule.

- **Foreground:** MR monogram in Electric Orange (#FF6B00), centered both horizontally and vertically. The monogram should fill approximately 60% of the icon's width.
- **No text:** The wordmark is never used in app icons. The monogram alone serves as the app identity.
- **No border:** The icon's shape itself is the boundary. No additional stroke or border.
- **Size deliverables:** 1024×1024px (source), with platform-specific exports at 16×16, 20×20, 29×29, 32×32, 40×40, 48×48, 50×50, 55×55, 57×57, 58×58, 60×60, 64×64, 72×72, 76×76, 80×80, 87×87, 88×88, 100×100, 114×114, 120×120, 128×128, 144×144, 152×152, 167×167, 180×180, 192×192, 256×256, 512×512, 1024×1024.

### Merchandise

- **Apparel:** MR monogram in Electric Orange on Deep Black or Charcoal garments. The monogram may be screen-printed, embroidered, or heat-transferred. Placement: left chest (primary), center chest (alternative), or full back (athletic jerseys).
- **Water bottles, shaker cups, gym bags:** MR monogram in Electric Orange. Single-color marking. No gradients (merchandise manufacturing constraints).
- **Notebooks, clipboards, coaching accessories:** Full logo lockup (horizontal) in monochrome White on Deep Black cover material. Deboss or screen print.
- **Event banners and signage:** Full logo lockup at the largest size permitted by the banner dimensions, maintaining minimum clear space. Electric Orange monogram on Deep Black background. No other brand elements on the same banner face.

### Co-Branding

When MR Training appears alongside partner brands or in integration contexts:

- MR Training logo must be equal in visual weight to partner logos. Never smaller, never larger.
- Minimum 2x the standard clear space between MR Training logo and partner logos.
- When displayed in a grid of logos (partners, integrations, "as seen in"), MR Training logo uses the monochrome variant appropriate to the background.
- In integration documentation (e.g., "Works with Garmin," "Powered by Stripe"), the partner logo appears at equal size, to the right of the MR Training logo, separated by a vertical 1px divider in `--color-surface-6`.
